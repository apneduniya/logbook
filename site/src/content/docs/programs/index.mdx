Logbook tracks coding time on a teen's own computer, decides which minutes count, and lets your program check that result. This guide is for the people who run a program and the developers who connect it.

## What your program gets

- **Counted hours, not raw editor time.** The agent on the teen's computer counts a minute only when someone was at the keyboard or an AI agent they started was working. Idle time is cut. See [What counts](/users/what-counts/) for the rules teens see.
- **The same API as Hackatime.** The calls programs already make (`authenticated/me`, `users/{id}/stats`, `trust_factor` and the rest) answer in Hackatime's shapes. Most programs switch by changing a base URL and registering a new OAuth app.
- **Submissions with evidence.** A teen sends a project's hours to your program. Logbook re-checks every minute on its server against signed logs from the teen's device, then gives you a review link that shows the hours, what was cut and why.

## One app is one program

On Logbook, a program is an **app**. You register it once at [logbook.boogles.dev/apps/new](https://logbook.boogles.dev/apps/new), and it does two jobs:

- **Sign-in and reading hours.** The app has a client id, a client secret and redirect URIs, like any OAuth app. Teens allow it to read their profile and activity.
- **Taking submissions.** If you tick **Take submissions**, the app is listed as a program. Teens can send it their hours with `--program <slug>`, and you and your reviewers decide on them.

Your apps, and the apps you review for, are on the **Apps** page (in the account menu).
