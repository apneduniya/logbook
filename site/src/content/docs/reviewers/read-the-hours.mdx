The HOURS panel is the first thing to read. It gives three figures for the same project and the same days, in hours with one decimal, like your program's records.

<Shot name="web-review-hours.png" alt="The HOURS panel: counted hours as the biggest figure, then at the keyboard and the one-third AI rule, with the evidence sentence and a VERIFIED tag under them" />

## The three figures

**Counted** is the number Logbook stands behind. It's the time the submitter was actually present and working, after Logbook removed what clearly wasn't effort: idle time, time an AI agent worked while they were away, and a few patterns described below. This is the number you approve or adjust.

**At the keyboard** is raw editor time, measured the way Hackatime measures it, from editor plugin heartbeats. It's what the submitter would have claimed with Hackatime.

**One-third AI rule** takes the time at the keyboard and counts the time editor plugins marked as AI coding at a third. It's there so you can compare Logbook's number with the flat rule many programs use today.

The panel's header shows the rules version, such as `rules v1`. The rules are public: see [What counts](/users/what-counts/).

### Reading them together

- **Counted is well below at the keyboard.** Logbook took a lot off. Look at CUTS AND FLAGS to see what and when.
- **Counted is close to at the keyboard.** The submitter was present for nearly all their editor time.
- **Counted is above at the keyboard.** This can happen. Logbook credits time spent prompting and watching an AI agent in a terminal or AI app, which editor plugins don't record as keyboard time.
- **Counted is below the one-third AI rule.** Logbook is stricter than the flat rule for this submission.

## MADE OF

Under the hours, a bar splits counted time into three parts:

- **Editing:** the submitter typed in the project's files that minute.
- **With AI:** they sent a prompt, answered an AI tool's question, or watched an agent work on the project.
- **Other:** counted, but with neither in the logs sent, such as reading, testing or terminal work.

This split is for understanding only. It never changes how much is counted.

## CUTS AND FLAGS

This panel reads like an invoice. Each line is a rule in plain words, the time it affects on the right, and when it happened underneath, as UTC time ranges. Long lists show the first few ranges and a **Show all** button. The short code at the end of each line (such as `agent_alone`) is the rule's name in the public rules.

<Shot name="web-review-cuts.png" alt="The CUTS AND FLAGS panel: a Cut group with lines such as idle and AI agent working alone, each with time taken off and UTC ranges, then a Flagged group with lines such as low signal and the minutes affected" />

**Cuts** were taken off the counted time. They're already out of the counted figure.

| Code | What it means |
|---|---|
| `idle` | Nobody was at the keyboard and no AI agent was working. |
| `agent_alone` | The AI agent worked while the person was away. |
| `rubber_stamp` | Three or more short approvals in a row ("yes", "continue"), each within 15 s of the agent finishing, with no editing between them. |
| `loop` | The same prompt sent three or more times within 30 minutes with no editing between them. |
| `background_editor` | Editor heartbeats with no writing or cursor movement for over 5 minutes, on a device with no input signal. |

**Flags** took nothing off. They point you at minutes worth a look.

| Code | What it means |
|---|---|
| `scripted_input` | Input or heartbeat timing too regular for a person. |
| `automated_session` | AI turns started with no input at the keyboard before them, such as scheduled or scripted runs. |
| `otel_mismatch` | Claude Code measured far less active time than the minutes credited in its sessions. |
| `long_day` | More than 12 credited hours in one day, in the submitter's own time zone. |
| `low_signal` | Credited without an operating system input signal, from editor activity alone. |
| `multi_device` | Time credited on two or more devices in the same minute. |

<Aside title="Flags are questions, not verdicts">
A flag says a pattern looks unusual. It doesn't say anyone cheated. Find the flagged ranges on the [day strips](/reviewers/days-and-ai-sessions/) and look at what was going on around them, such as commits and AI sessions, before you decide.
</Aside>

"Nothing was cut or flagged" means every minute of active time was counted.
